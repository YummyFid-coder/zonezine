What does the Teto skin look like?

Teto brings her distinctive drill-shaped twin tails into Fortnite. Use the official promotional image above and the linked gameplay showcase below to compare her appearance before buying. Promotional art is useful for identifying the character, while footage is better for judging movement, proportions and how an Outfit reads during play. Neither an illustration of Teto from another product nor a fan-made Fortnite concept is proof of the released skin. When comparing images, pay attention to the source and publication date, especially if you found them through an old social post.

How to get the Outfit

Open Fortnite on the Epic account where you want to keep your cosmetics, then look for the TWINDRILL DIVA Music Mini-Pass in the passes area. Review the reward preview and purchase confirmation rather than assuming that a similarly named shop offer contains the same items. If you already have an active Crew subscription, check whether the pass is accessible before spending V-Bucks. Once access is granted, look for Teto in your Locker. Additional rewards have their own progression requirements; buying access does not mean that every slot has already been completed.

Base Outfit versus the reactive style

The base character and the final music-reactive option are different milestones. Epic identifies the reactive style as the last tier reward. That matters if the glowing presentation in a clip is the reason you want the skin: check the final reward preview and plan time to progress through the pass. Do not interpret a video switching between appearances as evidence that both are delivered immediately. After earning a style, inspect the Outfit’s edit options in your Locker. The in-game preview is the best place to see which presentation choices your account currently owns.

What you are paying for

The advertised 1,000 V-Bucks is the pass price, rather than a separately confirmed standalone Item Shop price for Teto. Treat a pass purchase and a future shop purchase as different offers. For a useful comparison, list the rewards you actually want: the Outfit, a particular accessory, or music you will play. Someone who only wants the character may judge the offer differently from a Festival player collecting tracks. Our price guide breaks down those purchase decisions, while the rewards guide gives you a checklist to compare against the pass screen.

Before you confirm a purchase

Check the account name, current balance, selected pass and the contents shown on the confirmation screen. If the Outfit does not appear afterwards, check your transaction history and Locker before buying anything again. Avoid external offers promising a cheap account with the skin already attached: this guide only describes the official in-game purchase route.
